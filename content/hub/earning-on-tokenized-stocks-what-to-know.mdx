The live rates are on the [Earn page](/earn). This is what to read before acting on any of them.

## Base is the only yield that is really yours

Base APY is interest that borrowers pay. It is the part that survives when an incentive programme
ends. On tokenized stock markets it is currently close to zero almost everywhere, because very few
people want to borrow against a tokenized share.

## A big number usually means borrowed risk

The highest rates usually come from vaults that can deposit your stock, borrow a stablecoin against
it and loop. Where they do, that multiplies an incentive programme — and adds a liquidation price an
ordinary share has never had. Check each vault's allocation: some hold the stock and nothing else.

## Borrowing is the bigger opportunity

Lending a tokenized stock out pays almost nothing, but posting it as collateral and borrowing
against it is what most of these venues are built for. See the [borrow comparison](/borrow) for max
LTV and the cost of each loan.

## Incentives stop, and we keep the receipts

When a programme ends we do not delete the row. It stays with its last known rate, marked Ended, and
it is excluded from every headline figure on StockAPY. Crypto.com paid 2% to 4% on its tokenized
stocks and then stopped: the clearest evidence there is that an incentive is not a yield.

## A rate with no stated source is not interest

Centralised platforms publish a flat number without saying whether it is interest, a marketing
budget or revenue share. We show the rate and leave the split blank rather than guessing, and those
venues are excluded from the share-of-real-interest figure.

## Collateral is not earning

Several venues accept tokenized stocks but never lend them out. Your position unlocks borrowing power
and nothing else. Those rows say 0% because 0% is the truth, not because the data is missing.
