said. The answer is unflattering for the whole category, so it is worth stating plainly.

## The median tokenized stock earns nothing

Across every lending market that accepts a tokenized stock on the supply side, the median rate
is zero. Not "low" — zero. On 19 October 2026, Kamino's xStocks Market paid 0.19% on QQQx,
0.08% on SPYx, 0.02% on NVDAx and exactly 0.00% on everything else. Jupiter Lend's eight xStocks
vaults all read 0.00%.

This is not a data problem. It is what a lending market looks like when nobody borrows. Base APY
is interest that borrowers pay; with no borrow demand there is no interest. Tokenized equities
have very little borrow demand because there is no established shorting flow, the collateral
gaps between market sessions, and the pools are small.

## The big numbers are incentives wearing a yield costume

The headline rates in the table — 15% to 24% — all come from one place: Superform's SuperVaults
on Base. Take superTSLA, which showed 23.90% on 19 October 2026. Its base APY was **0.00%**. The
entire rate was token incentives.

Those vaults also do something a lending deposit does not: they borrow USDC against your stock
on Aave and loop the position. That amplifies the incentive, and it attaches a liquidation price
to an asset that, held directly, simply cannot be liquidated.

Both facts can be true at once. The yield is really being paid today, and it is a marketing
budget with a liquidation risk attached. Our table splits every rate into base and reward so the
difference is visible before you click through.

## Collateral venues are not earning venues

A second group of venues — Aave V4's Equities Hub, the Morpho Blue equity markets — accept
tokenized stocks and pay nothing by design. Your position is collateral; it is never lent out,
so there is no interest to receive. The interest in those markets flows to whoever supplied the
USDC on the other side.

We show these rows at 0% rather than hiding them, because "this venue exists and pays you
nothing" is useful information. It is also the single most common misunderstanding we ran into
while building this.

## What would have to change

For a tokenized stock to pay real interest, somebody has to want to borrow it. The plausible
paths are an onchain shorting market, basis trades against perpetual futures, or issuers passing
dividend economics through to token holders. None of those is meaningfully live today.

Until one of them is, the honest summary of this category is: tokenized stocks are a way to hold
equity exposure onchain, not a way to earn on it. Anyone quoting you a number above about 1%
should be asked which part is interest.
