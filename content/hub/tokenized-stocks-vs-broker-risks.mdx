Holding a tokenized stock keeps the price risk of the share and adds a few new risks. None of them
are reasons to avoid it outright, but each one should be a decision, not a surprise.

## Side by side

| Risk | Share at your broker | Tokenized stock |
|---|---|---|
| Price of the share | Yes | Yes |
| Who can fail | Your broker (client assets are usually segregated) | The token's issuer, and any platform you deposit into |
| Investor protection scheme | Usually, up to a limit | Generally none |
| Software risk | Low | Smart contracts can have bugs or be exploited |
| Losing access | Reset your password | Lose your wallet's recovery phrase and the tokens are gone |
| Prices outside market hours | No trading | Trades 24/7, can drift from the last close |
| Forced selling | Only on margin | On any loan, automatically, with no margin call first |

## Issuer risk

The token is a promise from its issuer. If the issuer cannot honour it, you are a creditor of that
company, not a registered owner of the share. Read how the issuer holds the shares and who audits
them. The [Issuers](/issuers) page links each one.

## Platform and smart-contract risk

Every place you deposit a token, a lending pool, a trading pool or a borrowing market, runs on
software called a smart contract. Bugs and exploits have drained platforms before. Older platforms
with audits and a long history are not risk-free, but have survived more.

## Your keys

In a self-custody wallet, you are the custodian. There is no support desk that can reverse a mistaken
transfer or restore a lost recovery phrase.

## Weekends and the price gap

The share stops trading on Friday; the token does not. Platforms still need a price, and when the
exchange reopens on Monday the real price can jump. If you have borrowed against the token, that jump
can liquidate the loan before you can react. See the lesson on [borrowing](/hub/borrowing-against-tokenized-stocks-risks).

## Taxes still apply

Moving a share into a token does not move it out of the tax system. Swaps, rewards and interest can
each be taxable events. Check the rules where you live.
