If you have bought a share through a broker, you already understand most of this. A tokenized stock
is the same price exposure, held in a different kind of account.

## The short version

A company (the **issuer**) buys real shares, or otherwise backs the token, and creates one token for
each share it holds. The token lives on a **blockchain**, a shared public ledger, instead of in your
broker's books. Its price follows the share.

You can think of it as a **tracker certificate**: you hold the issuer's promise to pay the value of
the share, and the issuer holds the shares to keep that promise.

## What you own, and what you don't

| | Share at your broker | Tokenized stock |
|---|---|---|
| Price exposure | Yes | Yes |
| Voting rights | Yes | Usually no |
| Dividends | Paid in cash | Depends on the issuer: reinvested, paid in tokens or not at all |
| Who you have a claim on | You are a registered shareholder (through your broker) | The issuer of the token |
| Trading hours | Exchange hours | Around the clock, every day |
| Where it can go | Stays in your account | Any compatible wallet or platform, in minutes |

The last two rows are the point. A token can move and be used outside market hours, on platforms
that do not need your broker's permission.

## Who issues them

Several companies issue tokenized stocks, each on its own blockchains and under its own terms.
The [Issuers](/issuers) page lists the ones we track. Two things differ between them and are worth
checking before you buy:

- **Who may hold them.** Most issuers do not offer their tokens to US residents. Read the issuer's
  terms for your country.
- **Which blockchain they live on.** A token on Solana cannot be used on a platform built on
  Ethereum or Base without moving it across, so the chain decides which of the opportunities on this
  site you can reach. Lesson 3 covers this.

## What a token lets you do

Once a share is a token, it can be put to work in three ways. Each has a familiar equivalent:

- **Lend it** and earn a rate: like a securities-lending programme. See [Lend](/earn).
- **Act as a market maker**: pair it with dollars in a trading pool and keep a fee on every trade.
  See [Market-make](/liquidity).
- **Borrow against it**: post it as collateral and borrow dollars without selling, like a margin or
  Lombard loan. See [Borrow](/borrow).

are often the least reliable.
